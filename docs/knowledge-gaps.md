# Knowledge gaps that set direction

v0.1 is biased towards data-rich taxa because that is what can be curated and
validated first. Data availability does not define the Atlas's scientific
scope. These gaps are recorded so that tractability does not quietly become
the project's boundary.

## The tropical and Southeast Asian frontier

Sesiidae have more than 1,500 described species, with the highest diversity
in the Afrotropical and Oriental realms. Every genome in
`releases/taxon_layers.tsv` and nearly every characterised pheromone comes
from Palaearctic or East Asian species. The Southeast Asian clearwings
studied for flight and acoustic mimicry of bees have, as far as this
snapshot knows, neither genomes nor identified pheromones.

## *Melittia*

*Melittia*, a large and largely tropical genus of bee-like clearwings, is a
natural discovery target for the questions in `docs/context.md`. Its
pheromone chemistry and genomes are not yet in the Atlas. Adding even one
characterised species would test whether the C18 2,13-/3,13-diene pattern
holds outside the temperate taxa.

## The tropical pheromone-trapping blind spot

Most sesiid attractants were found by screening synthetic C18 dienes in
temperate field trials. Species that do not respond to those compounds, or
that live where such screening has not been done, are invisible to that
method. An absence of trap records is therefore not an absence of species,
and not evidence about their chemistry.

## What would change this file

A genome assembly, a gland analysis or a field-screening study from any
tropical sesiid. Each becomes a row in the taxon × layer matrix, and the
corresponding gap here is narrowed or closed with a reference.
